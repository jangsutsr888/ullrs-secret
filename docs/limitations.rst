Limitations and field use
=========================

Ideal baseline
--------------

The model is best suited to clean snow in a wide-open alpine slope or bowl
with an unobstructed horizon. Departures from that baseline can move the
predicted transition time substantially.

Thermodynamic limits
--------------------

Linear degree-hours
   The integral treats a long period of weak warmth and a short flash of strong
   radiation as equivalent when their areas match. Snow structure may respond
   differently.

Surface model versus snow volume
   Effective temperature represents surface loading. It does not resolve heat
   storage through the full three-dimensional snowpack.

Latent heat and drainage
   Once snow becomes isothermal at the melting point, additional energy can go
   into phase change and runoff rather than higher snow temperature.

Subsurface heat flux
   A deep isothermal spring snowpack can return stored heat to the surface and
   delay an overnight reset. A cold winter base can have the opposite effect.

Terrain and microclimate limits
-------------------------------

Horizon masking
   The solar calculation includes slope self-shading but not ridges, trees,
   couloir walls, or a site-specific horizon. Complex terrain may lose sun
   earlier than the chart predicts.

Dirty snow
   The chart pipeline uses a fixed albedo of 0.7. Dust, pollen, ash, needles,
   and old dirty snow can absorb materially more shortwave radiation.

Wind
   The model does not explicitly include turbulent sensible or latent heat
   flux. Cold wind can accelerate surface cooling; warm downslope wind can
   supply heat that the curve misses.

Managed snow
------------

Grooming, skier traffic, and artificial snow change density, conductivity,
grain shape, and drainage. Thresholds calibrated for natural off-piste snow
should not be transferred directly to groomed pistes.

Operational interpretation
--------------------------

Use Ullr's Secret to compare candidate aspects, elevations, and timing, not to
declare a slope safe. Re-run the chart when the weather model changes, inspect
the overnight freeze in the field, and turn around when observations disagree
with the forecast.

The green corn band and crust-depth estimates do not establish avalanche
stability. Consult professional avalanche guidance and apply normal terrain
and travel protocols.
